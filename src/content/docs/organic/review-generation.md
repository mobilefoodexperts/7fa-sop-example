## What

A review generation system is the single highest-ROI activity in local SEO. More reviews = higher map pack rankings = more calls. This SOP covers the full system: when to ask, how to ask, what tool to use, how to handle the QR card program for field-based businesses, and what to do when a negative review lands. Most agencies set this up in Week 1 of onboarding and let it run on autopilot.

**The rule:** Ask at the moment of peak satisfaction — the second a customer says "great job" or signs off on a completed service call. That window is 15 minutes wide. After that, the likelihood of a review drops by 60%+.

## Who Owns It

**Account Manager** sets up the system and trains the client's field team. **Client owner or field tech** makes the ask in real time. The Account Manager monitors weekly and flags any gaps or negative reviews within 24 hours.

## Steps

### Step 1 — Set Up the Review Request Infrastructure

**Option A — SMS via GHL (preferred for clients on GoHighLevel)**

1. Create a GHL workflow: trigger = job closed / invoice paid
2. Send SMS immediately after trigger: `"Hi [first name], thanks for trusting us with [service]. Would you mind leaving us a quick Google review? It takes 30 seconds and means a lot. [SHORT LINK]"`
3. Use a shortlink that goes directly to the Google review form (not the GBP homepage). Get the direct link: search the business on Google → click "Write a review" → copy that URL → run through Bitly or GHL link tool
4. Set a 48-hour follow-up SMS if no review detected (manual check or use a review monitoring tool)

**Option B — QR Code Cards (essential for field-based businesses)**

For clients where techs visit customer locations (HVAC, fire protection, pest control, landscaping, etc.) — physical QR cards outperform digital requests with older demographics.

1. Create a QR code that links directly to the Google review form (same direct URL as above)
2. Print on 3.5" × 2" cards — design should include: logo, "How did we do? Scan to leave a review", QR code, star graphic
3. Print minimum 100 cards per tech
4. Script for techs: *"Before I head out — if everything looked good today, I'd really appreciate a Google review. It's just a scan and 30 seconds. Helps us out a lot."* Hand them the card.
5. Reorder when stock hits 20 cards

**Option C — Email (for service businesses with customer email lists)**

1. Use GHL or Mailchimp trigger: job status → completed
2. Send within 1 hour of job completion
3. Subject: `How did we do, [first name]?`
4. Body: 3 sentences max, one link, one CTA. No fluff.

---

### Step 2 — Train the Client's Team

The best review system fails if the field team doesn't use it. Run a 15-minute training call with whoever interacts with customers.

**What to cover:**
- Why reviews matter in plain language: "More reviews = you show up higher on Google Maps = more calls coming in without paying for ads"
- The exact moment to ask: when the customer says something positive, right before leaving
- The exact words to use (give them the script above — do not make them improvise)
- How to handle a customer who says they don't have a Google account: "No worries at all — just wanted to offer"
- What NOT to do: never offer incentives, never ask multiple times, never ask for "5 stars" specifically

---

### Step 3 — Handle Negative Reviews (within 24 hours)

A negative review handled well can actually improve trust. An ignored negative review compounds damage.

**Response framework for any negative review:**

1. **Respond publicly within 24 hours** — always, even if the review is unfair
2. **Acknowledge, don't argue:** "Thank you for sharing this — we're sorry the experience didn't meet your expectations."
3. **Move it offline:** "We'd like to make this right. Please call [number] or email [email] and ask for [name]."
4. **Never:** argue facts publicly, call out inaccuracies in the response, offer refunds publicly, ask them to remove the review in the public response

**If the review appears to be fake or from a competitor:**
1. Flag for Google review removal: Business Profile Manager → Reviews → Flag as inappropriate
2. Document with screenshots
3. If response from Google is no removal, respond publicly: "We couldn't find a record of this visit — if you're a real customer, please reach out directly so we can resolve this."

**Alert rule:** Account Manager gets notified of any review under 4 stars within 24 hours. Client gets notified same day with a suggested response draft.

---

### Step 4 — Monitor and Report Weekly

**Minimum weekly check:**
- [ ] How many new reviews this week?
- [ ] Average star rating (current vs. 30 days ago)?
- [ ] Any unanswered reviews (positive or negative)?
- [ ] Any reviews flagged for removal?

**Include in the weekly client report:**
- Review count this week vs. last week
- Current total review count and average rating
- Any flagged issues

**Monthly goal-setting:**
Set a realistic monthly review target with the client in the kickoff call. For a business with 0–20 reviews, target 10+ new reviews in the first 90 days. For a business with 50+ reviews, target 5–8 per month sustained.

---

### Step 5 — Competitor Benchmarking

Once per quarter, run a review gap analysis:

1. Search the top 3 competitors in the client's map pack
2. Log their review count and average rating
3. Calculate the gap: how many reviews does the client need to match or beat each competitor?
4. Adjust the monthly target accordingly

Share this in the QBR: "Competitor A has 187 reviews at 4.8 stars. You're at 43 reviews at 4.9 stars. At your current pace, you close the gap in 11 months — here's how we'd accelerate that."

---

## Definition of Done

- Review request system live and tested (SMS, QR cards, or email — at least one channel active) within Week 1 of onboarding
- Client field team trained with the exact script
- Negative review response protocol shared with client
- Weekly review monitoring active
- Monthly review target set and tracked in reporting dashboard
- After 90 days: client has minimum 10 new reviews posted since onboarding
